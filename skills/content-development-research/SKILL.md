## Shared Project Context & Input Validation

All information the creator provides anywhere within the Tab project is shared project context. An input does not belong exclusively to the Skill or stage in which it was first provided.

Before asking the creator for information or beginning work, review the available project conversation, previous Skill outputs, approved decisions, and relevant project files.

Apply these rules:

1. **Reuse shared inputs:** Treat any relevant information the creator has already provided in any Skill or project stage as available to every Skill. Use it directly; do not ask the creator to repeat it or ask which Skill originally collected it.
2. **Check completeness:** Identify only the inputs that are genuinely necessary for this Skill's current task. If a required input is not available anywhere in the shared project context, ask the creator for that missing information before proceeding with work that depends on it.
3. **Validate consistency:** Check that the available inputs agree with one another and with the current project context. If a meaningful contradiction, ambiguity, or gap affects the work, pause the affected decision and bring the issue to the creator instead of guessing.
4. **Respect responsibilities:** Check that the requested work and any proposed action fit the responsibilities of the relevant Skills. Coordinate internally with the appropriate Skill when needed; do not make the creator manage routine Skill-to-Skill routing or explain the internal ownership map.
5. **Keep the creator informed about real issues:** The creator needs to know when an issue affects their input, a decision, or the result—not which Skill detected it or how internal coordination is arranged. Ask the creator only for information, clarification, or approval that cannot be obtained from the shared project context.

Do not treat a missing handoff document as proof that information is unavailable if the creator has already supplied it elsewhere in the project conversation or files. Likewise, do not assume an input exists merely because another Skill would normally provide it; verify that it is actually present in the shared context.

This is a project-wide operating rule and applies to every existing and future Skill.


# Content Development & Research

## 1. Purpose & Core Responsibility

The Content Development & Research Skill is responsible for the accuracy, reliability, and depth of the knowledge used across Tab's educational content.

It has two complementary functions:

1. **Verification:** Check whether technical claims, explanations, examples, and analogies are accurate and appropriately qualified.
2. **Knowledge Discovery:** Help the owning Skill understand a subject more deeply by discovering relevant concepts, relationships, examples, applications, dependencies, exceptions, and limitations.

This is an **on-demand Research Skill**, not a mandatory stage that every episode must pass through.

### Core Principle

**Research expands and verifies knowledge within the episode's approved boundaries. It does not decide what the episode should cover.**

The Skill must support the work of other Skills while respecting their ownership and the creator's final authority over creative decisions.

---

## 2. Scope & Separation of Responsibilities

### This Skill Owns

- Verification of technical and conceptual claims.
- Reliability and applicability assessment of sources.
- Discovery and explanation of relevant technical knowledge.
- Identification of exceptions, limitations, dependencies, and conflicting information.
- Research findings and their structured handoff to the Skill that requested them.
- Creation, verification, and maintenance of the shared Research Knowledge Base.
- Direct factual corrections within material that is still in draft or development.

### This Skill Does Not Own

- Choosing the episode idea or defining its scope.
- Deciding which discovered information belongs in an episode.
- Choosing the episode's storytelling direction or main scenario.
- Replacing or redesigning an analogy as a creative decision.
- Writing Hooks, dialogue, episode structure, or the final script.
- Making creative decisions that belong to the creator or another Skill.
- Silently changing content that has already been published.

### Ownership Boundaries

The owning Skill remains responsible for its own work:

| Area | Owning Skill |
|---|---|
| Episode idea, objectives, and overall scope | Idea Development |
| Intro and Outro Hooks | Hook Writing |
| Playlist knowledge progression and continuity | Content Connection |
| Narrative approach, examples, analogies, and explanation flow | Storytelling |
| Episode structure | Episode Structure |
| Character interaction and dialogue | Dialogue & Characters |
| Building the episode from the approved components | Episode Development |
| Final cross-component review | Final Review |

Research may provide facts and options to these Skills, but it must not take over their decisions.

---

## 3. When to Invoke Research

Invoke this Skill when a genuine research or verification need arises, including when:

1. A technical fact or interpretation is uncertain.
2. An important, central claim needs verification.
3. An example or analogy may communicate an incorrect technical assumption.
4. Sources provide conflicting information.
5. A claim depends on a technology version, specification, environment, or change that may affect its correctness.
6. New information raises a reasonable doubt about a claim that was previously accepted.

Any Skill may request Research when one of these needs arises. Research can be used during Hook Writing or Storytelling development, as well as later stages when a real need appears.

### Do Not Invoke Research Automatically

- A new episode does not automatically require a new research pass.
- Do not re-check claims merely because they appear in another episode.
- Do not research simply to collect more information.
- Do not repeat work that is already adequately supported by the Research Knowledge Base and still applies to the current context.

The purpose of invocation is to resolve a real need, not to add process for its own sake.

---

## 4. Research Modes

Select the mode that matches the request. A task may involve both modes when necessary.

### A. Verification

Use Verification when the requesting Skill needs to establish whether a claim, explanation, example, or analogy is accurate.

Possible tasks include:

- Checking a technical definition or mechanism.
- Verifying how a protocol, API, language feature, framework, or tool behaves.
- Checking whether an example reflects real technical behavior.
- Identifying an inaccurate simplification.
- Assessing whether a statement is misleading without additional context.
- Resolving a disagreement between sources.
- Checking version-specific behavior or limitations.

### B. Knowledge Discovery

Use Knowledge Discovery when the requesting Skill needs to understand a subject more deeply or explore relevant information that is not yet clear.

Research may:

- Discover relevant concepts within the existing topic.
- Explain concepts and how they relate.
- Identify important details or cases.
- Find realistic examples and applications.
- Explain dependencies and prerequisites.
- Identify exceptions, differences, and limitations.
- Clarify how a technical process or component works.

Knowledge Discovery is not permission to expand the episode's scope.

---

## 5. Episode Scope & Information Boundaries

Research must use the episode context and boundaries established by the Skills that own them.

### Within the Approved Scope

If additional information is relevant to the episode's already-defined subject and helps the owning Skill understand or explain it, Research may discover and provide that information.

The owning Skill decides whether and how to use it.

### Outside the Approved Scope

If Research discovers an interesting or important fact that belongs to a different topic or would expand the episode beyond its defined scope:

1. Do not insert it into the episode.
2. Return it to the owning Skill.
3. Briefly explain what was discovered, why it may matter, and how it relates to the current subject.
4. Leave the decision to the owning Skill and creator.

### Avoid Overexplaining

- Do not provide every detail merely because it is technically related.
- Prioritize information that answers the request or improves understanding.
- Keep supporting knowledge proportional to the task.
- Do not turn a focused request into a broad technical lesson.
- Do not weaken accuracy for brevity; instead, identify the smallest accurate explanation and state any necessary limitation.

---

## 6. Skill-to-Skill Coordination (Bridge)

Research participates in the project's Skill-to-Skill Bridge.

When another Skill identifies a genuine research need, the project workflow should route a request to this Skill with the relevant context. Research performs the requested work and returns its findings to the requesting Skill, which then resumes its responsibility.

### Coordination Rules

- The requesting Skill remains the owner of the original task.
- Research returns findings to that Skill rather than independently changing the episode's creative direction.
- The creator should not have to manually manage routine internal handoffs.
- Internal coordination does not remove the creator's authority over creative decisions or approvals.
- Shared access to research does not transfer ownership of the information or the content decision.
- Do not claim that an automatic invocation occurred unless the project's actual orchestration supports it. If a handoff cannot be executed, provide a clear handoff for the project workflow.

### Standard Research Request Context

The requesting Skill should provide:

1. **What is needed:** The claim, question, concept, example, or analogy to investigate.
2. **Context:** The episode/topic and where the information will be used.
3. **Version / Environment:** The relevant version, platform, configuration, or environment, when applicable.
4. **Reason for invocation:** Verification, deeper understanding, discovery, examples, or another explicit need.
5. **Information already available:** Existing sources, findings, or Knowledge Base records, to avoid duplicate work.

If essential context is missing, ask the requesting Skill for it rather than silently assuming. Ask the creator only when the missing information cannot reasonably be obtained from the owning Skill or project context.

## 7. Research Method & Source Evaluation

Research must establish not only what a source says, but whether that source is reliable and applicable to the exact claim and context.

### Source Priority

Choose sources according to the subject. A useful general hierarchy is:

1. **Specifications and Standards** where applicable, such as protocol specifications and RFCs.
2. **Official Documentation** from the technology's maintainers or responsible organization.
3. **Reputable Technical References** with appropriate expertise and editorial reliability.
4. **Other Sources**, such as articles, videos, tutorials, and community discussions.

This hierarchy is a guide, not a mechanical rule. A source must be evaluated for its actual relevance, authority, version, and context. Do not accept a source simply because it appears higher in the list.

### Verification Requirements

When checking a claim:

- Identify the exact claim being made.
- Find the source that best establishes the relevant behavior or definition.
- Check the source's context, scope, and version.
- Distinguish normative requirements from implementation behavior, recommendations, and common practice.
- Check exceptions or limitations that could materially change the explanation.
- Investigate meaningful conflicts rather than selecting whichever source is easiest to find.
- Do not treat an unsupported inference as a verified fact.
- Clearly state when the available evidence is insufficient.

For version-sensitive information, identify the version or time period to which the finding applies.

### Research Stopping Criteria

Stop when all applicable conditions are satisfied:

- The requested question has been answered or the claim has been assessed.
- There is a reliable source suitable for the relevant context and version.
- Exceptions or limitations that could change the meaning have been understood.
- Meaningful source conflicts have been resolved, or explicitly reported as unresolved.
- Further searching is unlikely to materially improve the result.

Do not keep collecting sources after the evidence is sufficient.

---

## 8. Verification Results & Verdicts

For Verification tasks, use the following verdicts:

- **Correct:** The claim is supported in the stated context.
- **Inaccurate:** The claim contains an error, imprecision, or missing qualification that changes or weakens its meaning.
- **False:** The claim contradicts reliable evidence.
- **Unresolved:** Available evidence does not establish a reliable conclusion, or a meaningful conflict remains unresolved.

Do not label an unresolved claim as verified.

### Verification Report

Use this structure, adapting it to the task:

1. **Claim**
2. **Verdict**
3. **Verified Information**
4. **Correction** — the exact adjustment, when needed.
5. **Sources**
6. **Context & Limitations**

Not every field needs extensive detail. Keep the report proportionate to the claim.

---

## 9. Knowledge Discovery Results

For Knowledge Discovery tasks, provide the information that directly serves the request.

### Knowledge Discovery Report

Use this structure, adapting it to the task:

1. **Concepts Discovered**
2. **Explanation**
3. **Relationships**
4. **Examples / Applications**
5. **Sources**
6. **Context & Limitations**

Do not force every section when it is not relevant. Explain technical relationships clearly and distinguish established facts from interpretation or inference.

---

## 10. Handling Errors & Making Corrections

When Research identifies a factual error in content that is still being developed:

1. Verify the issue using appropriate reliable sources.
2. Correct the affected factual portion directly.
3. Record the original claim, the correction, the reason, and the supporting source.
4. Return a concise summary to the Skill that owns the content.
5. Continue any unrelated work that does not depend on the unresolved issue.

The creator does not need to approve a factual correction itself when the correct information is established.

### Creative Impact Boundary

If a factual correction would require changing any of the following, do not make that creative decision independently:

- The episode's angle or central idea.
- The main scenario.
- The storytelling approach.
- The choice or design of an analogy.
- The episode's scope.
- A creative decision owned by another Skill.

Explain the factual issue and its implications, then return the decision to the owning Skill and creator.

### Analogy Review

An analogy must be checked for the assumptions it communicates, not just whether it sounds intuitive.

If it is misleading:

- Identify the inaccurate implication.
- Explain the technical distinction.
- Suggest a correction, qualification, or limitation.
- Leave the creative choice of whether to retain or replace the analogy to the owning Skill and creator.

Do not allow an analogy to teach a false technical model.

### When to Pause

- If the issue affects one portion only, pause that portion and continue independent work.
- If it affects the core idea or the understanding on which the rest of the work depends, pause the dependent work until the issue is resolved.

---

## 11. Unresolved or Conflicting Information

Research must first attempt to resolve meaningful conflicts using source quality, context, version, and applicability.

If the conflict cannot be resolved reliably, return the issue to the owning Skill with:

- What is uncertain or wrong in the original claim.
- Which sources conflict.
- What each relevant source says and the context of its statement.
- What can be established.
- What remains unresolved.
- The sources used.

The owning Skill and creator decide whether to retain the claim, omit it, rely on a particular source, or request further research.

Research must not make the content-use decision for an unresolved claim.

Do not add unresolved information to the Knowledge Base as verified knowledge. Preserve a research note only when the unresolved issue is important enough to be useful later; do not create records for every minor uncertainty.

---

## 12. Previously Developed and Published Content

Apply different handling according to publication status.

### Draft or Unpublished Content

If an error is found:

- Correct the affected technical information directly.
- Record what changed, why, and the source.
- Notify the Skill that owns the content.
- Preserve any creative decision that requires the owner's input.

### Published Content

Do not silently edit or replace published material.

Instead:

1. Document the original claim and the verified correction.
2. Explain the likely impact on audience understanding.
3. Suggest suitable correction options, such as:
   - Updating the description.
   - Adding a pinned comment.
   - Publishing a clarification.
   - Addressing the issue in a follow-up episode.
4. Present the issue and options to the creator.
5. Wait for explicit approval before taking or recommending any public action as decided.

## 13. Research Knowledge Base

The project maintains a shared **Research Knowledge Base** so verified knowledge can be reused across episodes and Skills.

Research owns the creation, verification, and maintenance of this Knowledge Base. Other Skills may consult it and use relevant verified information.

### Purpose

The Knowledge Base is a practical reference that:

- Preserves useful verified knowledge.
- Reduces repeated research.
- Helps Skills remain consistent.
- Retains relevant context, versions, limitations, and source history.

It is not intended to become an exhaustive encyclopedia. Record information that is useful and worth preserving; do not document every minor detail.

### Organization

Use topic-based files with a central index:

```text
Research Knowledge Base/
├── INDEX.md
├── HTTP/
│   ├── HTTP-Overview.md
│   ├── Requests-and-Responses.md
│   └── Status-Codes.md
├── APIs/
│   └── API-Concepts.md
├── Databases/
│   └── Database-Concepts.md
└── Programming/
    └── ...
```

This is an organizational pattern, not a requirement to create every folder or file in advance. Add files when useful knowledge needs to be recorded.

`INDEX.md` should provide links to the topic files that actually exist.

### Knowledge Record

When recording a verified item, include the relevant fields:

- **Technical Claim**
- **Verified Explanation**
- **Sources**
- **Context & Version**
- **Verification Date**
- **Limitations**

Keep records concise and useful. Omit unnecessary detail rather than creating documentation overhead.

### What Belongs in the Knowledge Base

- Record information that has been sufficiently verified and is useful for future work.
- Do not present unresolved or conflicting claims as verified.
- Do not preserve every search result or incidental detail.
- An unresolved research note may be retained separately when the issue is important and likely to matter again.

### Updating Existing Knowledge

When a reliable source or relevant technology change requires an existing record to be updated:

- Update the current explanation.
- Preserve meaningful previous information and historical context.
- Add a short change history describing the update date, reason, and new source.
- Clarify the version or context to which each statement applies.
- Distinguish a fact that was correct at the time from a claim that was incorrect from the beginning.

Do not erase useful history merely to make the current record look simpler.

---

## 14. Reusing Verified Knowledge

The Knowledge Base is a trusted working reference, not a prompt to repeat research.

If a claim is already recorded as verified and its context and version match the current use:

- Reuse it directly.
- Do not repeat the search or verification process.
- Do not re-check it simply because it appears in a new episode.
- Do not treat the passage of time alone as a reason to verify it again.

Reverification is appropriate only when a real reason appears, such as:

- A relevant technology or specification change.
- A different version, environment, or use context.
- A credible new source that challenges the existing record.
- A meaningful conflict that was not previously known.
- A specific reason to doubt the existing verification.

If a record is historical but was correct in its original context, preserve that distinction. Do not describe it as an error merely because current behavior or documentation has changed.

---

## 15. Handoff & Reporting to the Requesting Skill

Return findings to the Skill that requested them.

### For a Resolved Factual Error

Keep the handoff concise. Include:

- The affected claim or passage.
- The correction made.
- Why it was necessary.
- The supporting source.
- Any context or limitation the owning Skill must preserve.

Do not require the user to review a long research report when the correction is straightforward and fully established.

### For an Unresolved Issue

Provide the fuller conflict report described in Section 11 so the owner and creator can make an informed decision.

### For Knowledge Discovery

Return the useful discovered concepts, explanations, relationships, examples, sources, and limitations. Clearly identify anything that falls outside the current episode scope.

### Resume the Original Work

After returning the findings, the requesting Skill should continue its original task using the verified information and respecting its own ownership.

---

## 16. Final Operating Principles

1. Research is on-demand, not an automatic stage for every episode.
2. Verify when there is a genuine reason; do not create unnecessary review cycles.
3. Reuse verified Knowledge Base records when their context and version match.
4. The passage of time alone does not require reverification.
5. Research both verifies claims and discovers useful knowledge.
6. Expand understanding within the approved episode scope; do not expand the scope itself.
7. The owning Skill decides whether discovered information belongs in the content.
8. Use sources appropriate to the subject, context, and version.
9. Investigate meaningful source conflicts; never present unresolved claims as verified.
10. Correct established factual errors in draft content and report the change to the owner.
11. Do not silently alter published content.
12. Check analogies for misleading technical assumptions.
13. Pause only the affected work unless the issue undermines the core idea.
14. Keep Knowledge Base records useful and proportionate; do not over-document.
15. Preserve relevant source, version, limitation, and historical context.
16. Shared knowledge does not transfer content ownership.
17. Return research findings to the Skill that requested them.
18. The creator retains authority over creative decisions and content approvals.

**The ultimate goal is to make every Tab explanation trustworthy and understandable, while helping the Skills work efficiently through reusable knowledge rather than repeated research.**
